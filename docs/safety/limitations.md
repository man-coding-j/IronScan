# Limitations

- Current Core ML packages are deterministic engineering fixtures, not validated screening models.
- Live tongue alignment is an engineering fixture signal and must not be treated as validated tongue detection; manual framing is required during device testing.
- Physical color-reference targets and capture protocol are not validated.
- Camera and latency validation on two physical iPhones remains pending.
- Tongue appearance varies with illumination, device, hydration, motion, anatomy, and acquisition quality.
- Tongue appearance annotations such as pallor, coating, redness, and tooth marks are not validated anemia-screening labels and cannot recover tissue color hidden by opaque coating.
- A model trained on `TonguePale` predicts the source dataset's visual pallor annotation only; its probability is not a hemoglobin, ferritin, anemia, or iron-status measurement, and decision thresholds remain unvalidated.
- The external eye/nail/tongue prototype dataset uses augmented images and proxy/simulated labels; it is restricted to engineering fixtures and excluded from deployable risk-model training and validation.
- Repository folder names such as `anemic` and `non_anemic` are source-provided proxy classes. They are not interchangeable with laboratory-confirmed anemia, iron deficiency, hemoglobin, or ferritin labels.
- External eye and nail images lack reviewed participant-group metadata. The educational demo keeps deterministic appearance-similarity clusters within one split, reducing leakage from close variants while remaining unable to prove that one real participant does not cross clusters. Eye and nail metrics are therefore not participant-independent validation.
- The clinical Eye engineering fixture uses a small Italy/India cohort and a laboratory-hemoglobin screening label. It does not identify iron-deficiency anemia, and its cohort, capture-device, demographic, fairness, and external generalization limits have not been validated.
- Exported predictive uncertainty is a deterministic 30-mask classifier-head ensemble. It can flag variation across fixed dropout masks, but it is not a calibrated guarantee of correctness and repeated Core ML calls do not generate fresh random masks.
- Vision conjunctiva cropping depends on detectable face and eye landmarks and uses an experimental lower-eyelid ROI. It can fail with occlusion, glasses, extreme pose, closed eyes, framing, skin-tone or device-domain shifts, and has not been validated against manually annotated conjunctiva crops on physical devices.
- Triple/dual-wide constituent switching, ultra-wide macro activation, `0.2` nail torch intensity, `-0.5 EV` eye bias, near-range autofocus, close-up framing, focus stability, and image quality remain unverified across supported physical iPhone and iPad hardware.
- The `0.15 g` rolling user-acceleration variation threshold is an engineering motion heuristic. It is not calibrated against image blur and requires physical-device validation across hand-held and supported mounting conditions.
- MapKit nearby-care results are third-party directory matches and do not guarantee that a location offers CBC testing, accepts a specific insurance plan, or is currently open. Apple Maps metadata may omit fields or contain stale phone, website, and address details. Category-based hours and holiday text are visibly labeled prototype estimates rather than live availability; users must confirm services and hours directly with the location.
- Home history displays a local research optical-pattern index from engineering model output. It is not an iron, hemoglobin, ferritin, disease, or clinical-severity measurement.
- Inverse-variance fusion epsilon `0.001`, high-variance cutoff `0.15`, eye multiplier `1.2`, nail multiplier `0.9`, tongue multiplier `0.9`, and context thresholds `0.35/0.65` have not been clinically or externally validated. They are versioned research assumptions and must not be interpreted as clinical confidence or disease probability.
- The displayed “model consistency” percentage is derived from ensemble variance relative to the research high-variance cutoff. It is not calibrated clinical confidence or probability of correctness.
- Fatigue, cold-hands, and plant-based-diet selections can change follow-up guidance, but they do not change any Core ML output. Biological sex is shown separately and is not scored because no validated adjustment is available.
- Menstrual-history context is optional, stored locally, and used only for follow-up wording. It does not alter Core ML output or establish the cause or clinical effect of reported bleeding.
- “General Iron Wellness” content is broad education, not individualized nutrition or treatment advice. Vitamin C, calcium, coffee, and tea effects vary by meal composition and individual circumstances; the app intentionally makes no universal absorption multiplier claim.
- The personalized action plan is a deterministic research copy-selection rule based on the displayed optical category and optional questionnaire context. It has not been clinically validated, does not prescribe a re-scan interval, and cannot determine iron status, hemoglobin, ferritin, anemia, or treatment needs.
- The onboarding account screen is a visual prototype only. It does not create an account, submit credentials, or persist its email and password entries; password fields are cleared before continuing.
- Native Liquid Glass appearance requires iOS 26 or later. Devices running the supported iOS 18–25 fallback use translucent material and may not reproduce the same blending, morphing, or optical response.
- Hemoglobin labels alone do not establish iron-deficiency anemia.
Debug builds may display categories from engineering-fixture models after first-launch research consent and capture-quality checks pass for all three tissue steps. A persistent research-prototype footer remains visible in the active flow. These categories are unvalidated demonstration outputs, are not participant results, and remain disabled in non-debug builds.
